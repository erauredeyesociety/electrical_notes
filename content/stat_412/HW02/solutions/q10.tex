\textbf{Problem.} A scientist inoculates mice, one at a time, with a disease
germ until he finds 2 that have contracted the disease.

If the probability of contracting the disease is \(\frac{1}{6}\), what is the
probability that 7 mice are required?

The probability that 7 mice are required is \(\boxed{\phantom{0.0000}}\).

(Round to four decimal places as needed.)

\textbf{Solution.} For the second case to occur on mouse 7, exactly one of the
first six mice must contract the disease and mouse 7 must contract it:
\[
P=\binom61\left(\frac16\right)^1\left(\frac56\right)^5
\left(\frac16\right)
=\binom61\left(\frac16\right)^2\left(\frac56\right)^5.
\]
\[
P=0.0669795953\approx\boxed{0.0670}.
\]
