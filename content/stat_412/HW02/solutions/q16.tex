\textbf{Problem.} Suppose that the probabilities are 0.1, 0.4, 0.2, and 0.3,
respectively, that 0, 1, 2, or 3 power failures will strike a certain
subdivision in any given year. Find the mean and variance of the random
variable \(X\) representing the number of power failures striking the
subdivision.

Calculate the mean, \(\mu\).

The mean is \(\boxed{\phantom{0.00}}\). (Round to two decimal places as needed.)

\textbf{Solution.}
\[
\mu=E(X)=0(0.1)+1(0.4)+2(0.2)+3(0.3)=1.70.
\]
\[
E(X^2)=0^2(0.1)+1^2(0.4)+2^2(0.2)+3^2(0.3)=3.90.
\]
\[
\sigma^2=E(X^2)-\mu^2=3.90-(1.70)^2=1.01.
\]
\[
\boxed{\mu=1.70,\qquad \sigma^2=1.01}
\]
