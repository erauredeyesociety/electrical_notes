\textbf{Problem.} According to a study published by a group of University of
Massachusetts sociologists, about 66.8\% of the 20 million persons in this
country who take Valium are women. Assume this figure to be a valid estimate.

(a) Find the probability that on a given day the sixth prescription written by
a doctor for Valium is the first prescribing Valium for a woman.

(b) Find the probability that on a given day the sixth prescription written by
a doctor for Valium is the third prescribing Valium for a woman.

(a) The probability that on a given day the sixth prescription written by a
doctor for Valium is the first prescribing Valium for a woman is
\(\boxed{\phantom{0.0000}}\).

(Round to four decimal places as needed.)

\textbf{Solution.} Let \(p=0.668\) and \(q=1-p=0.332\).

(a) The first five prescriptions must be for men and the sixth for a woman:
\[
P=q^5p=(0.332)^5(0.668)=0.0026944298\approx0.0027.
\]

(b) Exactly two of the first five must be for women, and the sixth must be for a woman:
\[
P=\binom52p^2q^3p=\binom52(0.668)^3(0.332)^3
=0.1090796256\approx0.1091.
\]
\[
\boxed{\text{(a) }0.0027\qquad\text{(b) }0.1091}
\]
