\textbf{Problem.} The random variable \(X\), representing the number of errors
per 100 lines of software code, has the following probability distribution.
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}x&2&3&4&5&6\\\hline
f(x)&0.25&0.3&0.04&0.01&0.4\end{array}
\]
Use the following theorem to determine the variance of the random variable \(X\).

The variance of a random variable \(X\) is
\(\sigma^2=E(X^2)-\mu^2\).

The variance is \(\boxed{\phantom{0.00}}\). (Round to two decimal places as
needed.)

\textbf{Solution.}
\[
\mu=2(0.25)+3(0.30)+4(0.04)+5(0.01)+6(0.40)=4.01,
\]
\[
E(X^2)=4(0.25)+9(0.30)+16(0.04)+25(0.01)+36(0.40)=18.99.
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)=18.99-(4.01)^2=2.9099\approx2.91.
\]
\[
\boxed{\sigma^2=2.91}
\]
