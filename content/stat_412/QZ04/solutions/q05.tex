\section*{Problem}
The joint density is
\[
f(x,y)=\frac4{27}x^2,\qquad0<x<3,\quad0<y<3-x,
\]
and zero elsewhere. (a) Show that $X,Y$ are not independent. (b) Find
$P(X>0.3\mid Y=1.7)$, rounded to six decimals as needed.

For part (a), the four displayed choices use $f(x\mid y)=f(x,y)/h(y)$ and
state, respectively: (A) on $0<x<3-y$ it involves $y$; (B) on that support it
is a function only of $x$; (C) on $0<y<3-x$ it involves $x$; or (D) on
$0<x<3-y$ it is constant.

\section*{Part (a)}
First find the marginal of $Y$:
\[
h(y)=\int_0^{3-y}\frac4{27}x^2\,dx
=\frac4{27}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^{3-y}
=\frac4{81}(3-y)^3,\qquad0<y<3.
\]
Hence
\[
f(x\mid y)=\frac{f(x,y)}{h(y)}
=\frac{3x^2}{(3-y)^3},\qquad0<x<3-y.
\]
This involves $y$, so the variables are not independent.
\finalanswer{(a) Select Choice A and enter
$\dfrac{3x^2}{(3-y)^3}$.}

\section*{Part (b)}
When $y=1.7$, the upper endpoint is $3-y=1.3$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(X>0.3\mid Y=1.7)
&=\int_{0.3}^{1.3}\frac{3x^2}{1.3^3}\,dx\\
&=\frac{3}{1.3^3}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{0.3}^{1.3}\\
&=\frac{1.3^3-0.3^3}{1.3^3}\\
&=\frac{2.197-0.027}{2.197}\\
&=\frac{2170}{2197}\approx0.987711.
\end{aligned}
\]
\finalanswer{(b) $\dfrac{2170}{2197}\approx0.987711$.}
