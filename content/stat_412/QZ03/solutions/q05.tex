\section*{Problem}
A certain type of device has an advertised failure rate of $0.01$ per hour. The
failure rate is constant and the exponential distribution applies. What is the
probability that less than 500 hours will elapse before 6 failures occur?
(Use the incomplete gamma table.)
\textit{(Round to three decimal places as needed.)}

\section*{Setup}
Failures arrive at a constant rate, so the \emph{waiting time between} successive
failures is exponential. The \emph{total} waiting time $T$ until the
\textbf{6th} failure is the sum of 6 such exponential waits, which is a
\textbf{gamma} random variable with
\[
\text{shape }\alpha=6,\qquad
\text{scale }\beta=\frac1{\lambda}=\frac1{0.01}=100\text{ hours.}
\]
($\beta$ is the mean time between failures.) We need $P(T<500)$.

\section*{Work}
The incomplete gamma table gives the cumulative value $F(x;\alpha)$ for the
\emph{standard} gamma (scale $\beta=1$). For a general scale $\beta$,
\[
P(T<t)=F\!\left(\frac{t}{\beta};\,\alpha\right),
\]
so we standardize the time by dividing by $\beta$:
\[
\frac{t}{\beta}=\frac{500}{100}=5 .
\]
Reading the table at row $x=5$, column $\alpha=6$:
\[
P(T<500)=F(5;6)=0.384 .
\]

\finalanswer{$P(T<500)=F(5;6)=\mathbf{0.384}$.}
