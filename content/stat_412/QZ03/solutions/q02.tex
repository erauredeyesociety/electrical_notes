\section*{Problem}
The shelf life of a product is a random variable that is related to consumer
acceptance. The shelf life $Y$ in days of a certain bakery product has the
density function below. What fraction of the loaves stocked today would you
expect to be sellable 2 days from now?
\[
f(y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac27\,e^{-2y/7},&0\le y<\infty,\\
0,&\text{elsewhere.}
\end{cases}
\]
\textit{(Round to four decimal places as needed.)}

\section*{Setup}
This is an \textbf{exponential} density, $f(y)=\lambda e^{-\lambda y}$, with rate
parameter $\lambda=\tfrac27$ per day (equivalently a mean shelf life of
$\tfrac1\lambda=\tfrac72=3.5$ days). For an exponential variable the
right-tail probability has the closed form
\[
P(Y>y)=e^{-\lambda y}.
\]

\section*{Work}
A loaf stocked today is still sellable in 2 days exactly when its shelf life
exceeds 2 days, so we want $P(Y>2)$:
\[
P(Y>2)=e^{-\lambda(2)}=e^{-(2/7)(2)}=e^{-4/7}\approx0.5647 .
\]

\finalanswer{$P(Y>2)=e^{-4/7}\approx 0.5647$ --- about $56.47\%$ of the loaves.}
