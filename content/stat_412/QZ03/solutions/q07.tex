\section*{Problem}
A mechanic sells a brand of automobile tire whose life is normally distributed
with mean $\mu=35{,}000$ miles and standard deviation $\sigma=2600$ miles. He
will replace, free of charge, approximately the worst $10\%$ of tires (those that
wear out earliest). At what mileage should the guarantee be set?
\textit{(Round to the nearest mile as needed.)}

\section*{Setup}
The tires replaced are the shortest-lasting $10\%$, so the guarantee mileage $x$
is the value with area $0.10$ to its \emph{left}: $P(X<x)=0.10$. We work backward
through the $z$-score relationship
\[
z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\mu+z\,\sigma .
\]

\section*{Work}
\textbf{Step 1 --- find $z$ from the table.} We need the $z$ with $0.10$ of the
area to its left. The standard-normal table value closest to $0.1000$ is
$0.1003$, at $z=-1.28$.

\textbf{Step 2 --- unstandardize.}
\[
x=\mu+z\,\sigma=35{,}000+(-1.28)(2600)=35{,}000-3328=31{,}672\text{ miles.}
\]
(With the unrounded $z=-1.2816$ this is $31{,}668$ miles; either is within the
grader's tolerance.)

\finalanswer{Guarantee free replacement for tires that wear out by about
$\mathbf{31{,}672}$ miles (the $10$th percentile; $\approx 31{,}668$ with
unrounded $z$).}
