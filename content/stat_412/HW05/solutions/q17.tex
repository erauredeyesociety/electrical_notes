\section*{Problem}
Two box-filling machines have product-weight variance $\sigma^2=5$. Samples of
size $64$ are taken from each machine. Part (a) asks for
$P(\bar X_B-\bar X_A\ge0.3)$ assuming $\mu_A=\mu_B$.
Part (b) asks whether this strongly supports different population means.

\section*{Work}
Let $D=\bar X_B-\bar X_A$. Under $\mu_A=\mu_B$,
\[
\mu_D=0,
\qquad
\sigma_D=\sqrt{\frac{5}{64}+\frac{5}{64}}
=\sqrt{\frac{10}{64}}\approx0.3953.
\]
Then
\[
z=\frac{0.3-0}{0.3953}\approx0.76.
\]
Using the z-table,
\[
P(D\ge0.3)=P(Z\ge0.76)=1-0.7764=0.2236.
\]
This probability is not negligible, so this sample difference is not especially
surprising when the population means are equal.

\finalanswer{(a) $0.2236$.
(b) Select: probability \textbf{is not} negligible; the experiments \textbf{do not} support the conjecture.}
