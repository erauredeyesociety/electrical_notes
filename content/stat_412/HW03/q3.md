# Question 3

An important factor in solid missile fuel is the particle size distribution.
Significant problems occur if the particle sizes are too large. From production
data in the past, it has been determined that the particle size (in
micrometers) distribution is characterized by the following function.

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
6x^{-7}, & x>1,\\
0, & \text{elsewhere}.
\end{cases}
\]

(a) Verify that this is a valid density function.

(b) Evaluate \(F(x)\).

(c) What is the probability that a random particle from the manufactured fuel
exceeds 2 micrometers?

## Part (a)

The function \(f(x)\) is a valid density function because **\(f(x)\ge 0\)** for
all **\(x\in\mathbb{R}\)** and because

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx
=\int_1^\infty 6x^{-7}\,dx
=\left(-x^{-6}\right)\Big|_1^\infty
=1.
\]

> **Grading note (`3-3.png`).** MyStatLab marked the first two dropdowns. The
> accepted answers are \(f(x)\ge 0\) (not \(f(x)>0\)) for all \(x\in\mathbb{R}\)
> (not \(x>1\)). A valid density must be non-negative everywhere on the real
> line, including where the piecewise rule sets it to \(0\).

## Part (b)

\[
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0, & x\le 1,\\
1-x^{-6}, & x>1.
\end{cases}
\]

## Part (c)

\[
P(X>2)=1-F(2)=2^{-6}=\tfrac{1}{64}\approx 0.0156.
\]
