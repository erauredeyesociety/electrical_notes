# Question 1

A study shows that running increases the percent resting metabolic rate (RMR) in older women. The average RMR of 25 elderly women runners was 34.5% higher than the average RMR of 25 sedentary elderly women, and the standard deviations were reported to be 10.3% and 10.2%, respectively.

Was there a significant increase in RMR of the women runners over the sedentary women? Assume the two populations are approximately normally distributed with equal variances. Use a P-value in the conclusion.

Let sample 1 be the women runners and let sample 2 be the sedentary women.

## Solution

- Hypotheses: \(H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0\), \(H_1:\mu_1-\mu_2>0\).
- Pooled standard deviation: \(s_p=10.2501\).
- Test statistic: \(t=11.90\) with \(df=48\).
- P-value: approximately \(0.000\).
- Conclusion: reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that runners have a higher mean RMR.
