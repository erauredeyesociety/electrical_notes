\section*{HW08 Question 8}

\textbf{Problem.} A study compares 1,502 first-grade students in classrooms using educational software with 1,115 first-grade students in classrooms not using the software. Let group 1 be software users and group 2 be non-users.

\textbf{Solution.}

The parameter compares the population mean test scores of the two groups:
\[
\boxed{\mu_1-\mu_2}.
\]
In words, this is the population mean test score for first-grade students using educational software minus the population mean test score for first-grade students not using it.

\textbf{Part (b).} The study concluded that mean test scores were higher in classrooms using the software, so the hypotheses are
\[
H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0,\qquad H_a:\mu_1-\mu_2>0.
\]

\textbf{Part (c).} The reported P-value is \(0.03\). A P-value this small means the sample result would be unusual if the null hypothesis were true. At the usual \(0.05\) level,
\[
0.03<0.05,
\]
so we reject \(H_0\). This supports the research group's conclusion that the software group had significantly higher mean test scores.

\[
\boxed{\text{Yes; there was a difference. Since the P-value is small, reject }H_0\text{ and side with the research group.}}
\]
