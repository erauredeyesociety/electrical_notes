\section*{HW08 Question 4}

\textbf{Problem.} Plasma ascorbic acid levels are compared for nonsmoking and smoking pregnant women. Let sample 1 be nonsmokers and sample 2 be smokers. Use unequal variances and a P-value to test whether the mean plasma ascorbic acid levels differ.

\textbf{Known setup.}
\[
H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0,\qquad H_1:\mu_1-\mu_2\ne0.
\]

\textbf{Data summary.} For nonsmokers,
\[
n_1=23,\qquad \bar{x}_1=0.9309,\qquad s_1=0.2059.
\]
For smokers,
\[
n_2=7,\qquad \bar{x}_2=0.9757,\qquad s_2=0.4228.
\]

\textbf{Test statistic.} Using Welch's two-sample test,
\[
t=\frac{(\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2)-0}{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}}
  =\frac{0.9309-0.9757}{\sqrt{0.2059^2/23+0.4228^2/7}}
  =-0.271.
\]
The Welch degrees of freedom are \(df\approx6.89\).

\textbf{P-value and conclusion.} Since this is a two-tailed test,
\[
p=2P(T_{6.89}\ge |{-0.271}|)\approx0.794.
\]
The P-value is large, so we fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence to conclude that the mean plasma ascorbic acid levels differ between nonsmokers and smokers.

\[
\boxed{t=-0.271,\quad df\approx6.89,\quad p\approx0.794;\ \text{do not reject }H_0.}
\]
