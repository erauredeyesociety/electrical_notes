\section*{HW08 Question 14}

\textbf{Problem.} Dog malaria-detection results:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{Malaria present} & \text{Malaria not present}\\ \hline
\text{Dog correct} & 123 & 131\\
\text{Dog wrong} & 52 & 14
\end{array}
\]
Find the chi-square statistic, P-value, and conclusion. Also determine whether rejecting independence implies that dogs are good at identifying malaria.

\textbf{Solution.}
\[
H_0:\text{dog correctness and malaria presence are independent.}
\]
\[
H_1:\text{dog correctness and malaria presence are not independent.}
\]
Using expected counts from row and column totals,
\[
\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}=19.49,\qquad df=(2-1)(2-1)=1.
\]
The P-value is \(p\approx0.000010\), so rounded to three decimals, \(p=0.000\).

\[
\boxed{\text{Reject }H_0.}
\]
There is sufficient evidence that correctness and malaria presence are not independent.

\textbf{Part (b).} No. Rejecting independence only shows that dog correctness and malaria status are associated. It does not, by itself, prove that dogs are good identifiers; an association could also occur in a direction that reflects poor performance.

\[
\boxed{\text{No; rejection of independence alone does not imply good identification.}}
\]
