\section*{HW08 Question 12}

\textbf{Problem.} Teenager opinion counts are Strongly agree 88, Somewhat agree 60, Neither 35, Somewhat disagree 15, Strongly disagree 2. Test at \(\alpha=0.01\) whether the distribution for teenagers differs from the parent distribution shown in a pie chart.

\textbf{Known setup.}
\[
H_0:\text{teenagers' distribution is the same as parents' distribution.}
\]
\[
H_a:\text{teenagers' distribution is different from parents' distribution.}
\]
The claim is \(H_a\).

\textbf{Solution.} Using the parent pie-chart proportions
\[
(0.52,0.29,0.08,0.07,0.04),
\]
the expected counts are
\[
200(0.52,0.29,0.08,0.07,0.04)=(104,58,16,14,8).
\]
\[
\chi^2=\sum\frac{(O_i-E_i)^2}{E_i}.
\]
\[
\chi^2
=\frac{(88-104)^2}{104}
+\frac{(60-58)^2}{58}
+\frac{(35-16)^2}{16}
+\frac{(15-14)^2}{14}
+\frac{(2-8)^2}{8}
=29.664.
\]
\[
df=5-1=4,\qquad p=P(\chi^2_4\ge29.664)\approx0.0000.
\]
Since \(p<0.01\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence to support the economist's claim.

Platform conclusion dropdowns:
\[
\boxed{\text{Reject};\quad \text{is};\quad \text{is different for teenagers and parents.}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\chi^2=29.664,\quad df=4,\quad p\approx0;\ \text{reject }H_0.}
\]
