\section*{HW08 Question 9}

\textbf{Problem.} Independent samples of size 400 are selected from two populations. Sample 1 has \(\bar{x}_1=5315,\ s_1=157\). Sample 2 has \(\bar{x}_2=5275,\ s_2=199\). Find a 95\% confidence interval for \(\mu_1-\mu_2\), rounded to one decimal place.

\textbf{Solution.}
\[
\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2=5315-5275=40.
\]
\[
SE=\sqrt{\frac{157^2}{400}+\frac{199^2}{400}}=12.6738.
\]
Using \(z_{0.025}=1.96\),
\[
40\pm1.96(12.6738)=40\pm24.8406.
\]
\[
\boxed{(15.2,\ 64.8)}.
\]
Interpretation choice C: We are 95\% confident that the difference between the population means falls in the confidence interval. In context, \(\mu_1-\mu_2\) is between 15.2 and 64.8.

\textbf{Hypothesis test.}
\[
H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0,\qquad H_a:\mu_1-\mu_2\ne0,\qquad \alpha=0.05.
\]
\[
z=\frac{5315-5275}{\sqrt{157^2/400+199^2/400}}
=\frac{40}{12.6738}=3.16.
\]
The two-tailed P-value is
\[
p=2P(Z\ge3.156)\approx0.0016.
\]
Because \(p<0.05\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that the two population means are different.

\[
\boxed{z=3.16,\quad p=0.0016,\quad \text{reject }H_0.}
\]

\textbf{Part (d): test against a difference of 26.} The added prompt asks to test
\[
H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=26,\qquad H_a:\mu_1-\mu_2\ne26,
\]
using \(\alpha=0.05\). The observed sample difference is still \(40\), so
\[
z=\frac{40-26}{12.6738}=1.10.
\]
The two-tailed P-value is
\[
p=2P(Z\ge1.1046)\approx0.2693.
\]
Since \(p>0.05\), fail to reject \(H_0\). This differs from the earlier test against 0: the data showed evidence that the difference is not 0, but not enough evidence that the difference is different from 26.

\[
\boxed{z=1.10,\quad p=0.269,\quad \text{fail to reject }H_0.}
\]

\textbf{Comparison choice.} The test against 0 rejects, so part (b)/(c) supported that the means are different. The test against 26 fails to reject, so in the platform's wording it supported that the difference is 26.
\[
\boxed{\text{Choice B.}}
\]

\textbf{Assumptions.} The procedures for independent-samples inference require
the two samples to be independent random samples. With \(n_1=n_2=400\), the
large sample sizes justify the normal approximation for \(\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2\).
\[
\boxed{\text{Choice D: the two samples are independent random samples.}}
\]
