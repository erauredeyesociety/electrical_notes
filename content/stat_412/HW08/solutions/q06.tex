\section*{HW08 Question 6}

\textbf{Problem.} A random sample of 90 adults is classified by gender and hours of television watched per week. At \(\alpha=0.01\), test whether time spent watching television is independent of gender.

\textbf{Known setup.}
\[
H_0:\text{TV time and gender are independent.}
\]
\[
H_1:\text{TV time and gender are not independent.}
\]

\textbf{Observed counts.}
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & \text{Male} & \text{Female} & \text{Total}\\ \hline
\text{Over 25 hours} & 15 & 28 & 43\\
\text{Under 25 hours} & 28 & 19 & 47\\ \hline
\text{Total} & 43 & 47 & 90
\end{array}
\]

\textbf{Expected counts.} Compute expected counts by
\[
E_{ij}=\frac{(\text{row total})(\text{column total})}{\text{grand total}}
\]
which gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{Male} & \text{Female}\\ \hline
\text{Over 25 hours} & 20.544 & 22.456\\
\text{Under 25 hours} & 22.456 & 24.544
\end{array}
\]

\textbf{Test statistic and conclusion.}
\[
\chi^2=\sum \frac{(O-E)^2}{E}=5.487,\qquad df=(2-1)(2-1)=1.
\]
\[
p=P(\chi^2_1\ge5.487)\approx0.0192.
\]
Since \(p>0.01\), fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence at the 0.01 level to conclude that TV time and gender are dependent.

\[
\boxed{\chi^2=5.487,\quad df=1,\quad p\approx0.0192;\ \text{do not reject }H_0.}
\]
