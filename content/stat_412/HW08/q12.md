# Question 12

The pie chart shows the distribution of the opinions of parents on whether a college education is worth the expense. An economist claims that the distribution of the opinions of teenagers is different from the distribution for parents. To test this claim, you randomly select 200 teenagers and ask each whether a college education is worth the expense. The table shows the results. At \(\alpha=0.01\), test the economist's claim.

| Response | Frequency |
|---|---:|
| Strongly agree | 88 |
| Somewhat agree | 60 |
| Neither agree nor disagree | 35 |
| Somewhat disagree | 15 |
| Strongly disagree | 2 |

## Known hypothesis setup

- \(H_0\): the distribution of teenagers' opinions is the same as the parents' distribution.
- \(H_a\): the distribution of teenagers' opinions is different from the parents' distribution. This is the claim.

## Solution

Using the parent pie-chart distribution

\[
(0.52,\ 0.29,\ 0.08,\ 0.07,\ 0.04)
\]

for strongly agree, somewhat agree, neither, somewhat disagree, and strongly disagree, the expected counts for \(n=200\) teenagers are

\[
(104,\ 58,\ 16,\ 14,\ 8).
\]

\[
\chi^2
=\frac{(88-104)^2}{104}
+\frac{(60-58)^2}{58}
+\frac{(35-16)^2}{16}
+\frac{(15-14)^2}{14}
+\frac{(2-8)^2}{8}
=29.664.
\]

With \(df=5-1=4\),

\[
p=P(\chi^2_4\ge29.664)\approx0.0000.
\]

Since \(p<0.01\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence to support the economist's claim that teenagers' opinions are distributed differently from parents' opinions.

## Platform conclusion dropdowns

- Dropdown options: **Fail to reject** / **Reject**. Select **Reject** \(H_0\).
- Dropdown options: **is** / **is not**. Select **is**.
- Dropdown options:
  - **is different for teenagers and parents.**
  - **is uniform for parents.**
  - **is uniform for teenagers.**
  - **is the same for teenagers and parents.**

Select **is different for teenagers and parents.**

Completed sentence: Reject \(H_0\). At the 1% significance level, there **is** enough evidence to conclude that the distribution of the opinions of whether a college education is worth the expense **is different for teenagers and parents.**
