# Question 20

In a study of high school students at least 16 years of age, researchers obtained survey results summarized in the accompanying table. Use a 0.05 significance level to test the claim of independence between texting while driving and driving when drinking alcohol. Are those two risky behaviors independent of each other?

## Hypothesis setup

- \(H_0\): texting while driving and driving when drinking alcohol are independent.
- \(H_1\): texting while driving and driving when drinking alcohol are not independent.

## Data

|  | Drove when drinking alcohol: Yes | Drove when drinking alcohol: No |
|---|---:|---:|
| Texted while driving | 725 | 3024 |
| No texting while driving | 159 | 4563 |

## Solution

The expected counts are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{Yes} & \text{No}\\ \hline
\text{Texted while driving} & 391.231 & 3357.769\\
\text{No texting while driving} & 492.769 & 4229.231
\end{array}
\]

\[
\chi^2=570.3387,\qquad df=1,\qquad p\approx4.74\times10^{-126}.
\]

Platform entry for the \(x^2\) / \(\chi^2\) value: **570.34** if the table below is exactly correct and the platform asks for two decimals.

Platform entry for the P-value: **0.000** if the platform asks for three decimals, or **0.0000** if it asks for four decimals.

Since \(p<0.05\), reject \(H_0\). Texting while driving and driving after drinking alcohol are not independent.
