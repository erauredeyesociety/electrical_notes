# Question 14

The following table lists results from an experiment designed to test the ability of dogs to use their extraordinary sense of smell to detect malaria in samples of children's socks. The accompanying information shows the hypotheses and TI-83/84 Plus calculator results for the experiment. Assume that the hypothesis test requirements are all satisfied.

| Result | Malaria was present | Malaria was not present |
|---|---:|---:|
| Dog was correct | 123 | 131 |
| Dog was wrong | 52 | 14 |

## Part (a)

Identify the test statistic and the P-value, and then state the conclusion about the null hypothesis.

## Part (b)

Does rejection of the null hypothesis of independence imply that dogs are good at identifying malaria?

## Solution

- Test statistic: \(\chi^2=19.49\).
- P-value: approximately \(0.000\) \((p\approx0.000010)\).
- Conclusion: reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that dog correctness and whether malaria was present are not independent.
- Part (b): No. Rejecting independence only shows an association between dog correctness and malaria status. By itself, that kind of rejection does not guarantee that dogs are good identifiers; it is possible to reject independence in a direction that would not indicate good performance.
