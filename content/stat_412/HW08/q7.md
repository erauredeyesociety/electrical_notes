# Question 7

The "fear of negative evaluation" (FNE) scores for 11 female students known to suffer from the eating disorder bulimia and 14 female students with normal eating habits are represented in the table attached to the problem. The higher the score, the greater the fear of a negative evaluation. A technology output analyzing this data is attached.

Complete parts (a) and (b).

## Technology output

Difference \(=\mu(\text{Bulimic})-\mu(\text{Normal})\).

| Group | \(n\) | Mean | StDev | SE Mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bulimic | 11 | 15.82 | 4.92 | 1.5 |
| Normal | 14 | 12.14 | 5.29 | 1.4 |

Estimate for difference: \(3.68\)

95% CI for difference: \((-0.60,\ 7.95)\)

T-test of difference \(=0\) vs not equal:

- \(T=1.79\)
- P-value \(=0.086\)
- \(df=23\)
- Pooled StDev \(=5.1303\)

## Solution

- (a) The 95% confidence interval is \((-0.60,\ 7.95)\).
- (b) Required assumptions for the interval to be statistically valid: **A, C, and D**.
  - A. The scores were randomly selected in an independent manner.
  - C. The FNE score distributions for both groups are approximately normal.
  - D. The variances of the two populations are equal.

Do **not** select B, because the sample sizes are \(n_1=11\) and \(n_2=14\), so both samples are not large.

Since the interval contains 0 and the P-value \(0.086>0.05\), there is not sufficient evidence of a difference between the population mean FNE scores.

## Part (c)

Are these assumptions reasonably satisfied?

Answer: **Yes, the assumptions are reasonably satisfied.**
