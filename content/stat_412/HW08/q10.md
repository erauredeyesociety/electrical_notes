# Question 10

A research firm claims that the distribution of the days of the week that people are most likely to order food for delivery is different from the distribution shown in the pie chart. You randomly select 489 people and record which day of the week each is most likely to order food for delivery. The table shows the results. At \(\alpha=0.01\), test the research firm's claim.

| Day | Frequency |
|---|---:|
| Sunday | 42 |
| Monday | 17 |
| Tuesday | 25 |
| Wednesday | 49 |
| Thursday | 41 |
| Friday | 165 |
| Saturday | 150 |

## Hypothesis setup

- \(H_0\): the distribution of the days people order food for delivery is as shown in the pie chart.
- \(H_a\): the distribution of the days people order food for delivery is different from the pie-chart distribution. This is the claim.

## Solution

Using the pie-chart percentages in day order Sunday through Saturday,

\[
(0.12,\ 0.02,\ 0.03,\ 0.14,\ 0.10,\ 0.31,\ 0.28),
\]

the expected counts for \(n=489\) are

\[
(58.680,\ 9.780,\ 14.670,\ 68.460,\ 48.900,\ 151.590,\ 136.920).
\]

\[
\chi^2=\sum \frac{(O-E)^2}{E}=26.589,\qquad df=7-1=6.
\]

\[
p=P(\chi^2_6\ge26.589)\approx0.000173.
\]

Rounded to three decimals, the P-value is **0.000**.

## Platform hypothesis dropdowns and pending fields

- H0: The distribution of the days people order food for delivery is as shown in the pie chart.
- Ha: The distribution of the days people order food for delivery differs from the expected distribution.
- Claim: \(H_a\).
- Test statistic: **26.589**.
- P-value: **0.000**.
- Decision: reject \(H_0\).

The alternative hypothesis is the claim.

At the 1% significance level, there is enough evidence to support the research firm's claim that the delivery-day distribution differs from the expected distribution.
