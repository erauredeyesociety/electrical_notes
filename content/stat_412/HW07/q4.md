# Question 4

A research report claims that mice with an average life span of 32 months will live to be about 41 months old when 40% of the calories in their diet are replaced by vitamins and protein. Is there any reason to believe that \(\mu<41\) if 68 mice that are placed on this diet have an average life of 40 months with a standard deviation of 3.4 months? Use a P-value in your conclusion.

Identify the null and alternative hypotheses.

Choices:

- A. \(H_0:\mu=41,\ H_a:\mu\ne41\)
- B. \(H_0:\mu<41,\ H_a:\mu=41\)
- C. \(H_0:\mu\ne41,\ H_a:\mu<41\)
- D. \(H_0:\mu=41,\ H_a:\mu>41\)
- E. \(H_0:\mu=41,\ H_a:\mu<41\)
- F. \(H_0:\mu>41,\ H_a:\mu=41\)

## Cleaned / confirmed parts

- Platform-confirmed hypothesis choice: **E**
  - \(H_0:\mu=41\)
  - \(H_a:\mu<41\)

Using the visible screenshot values \(n=68\), \(\bar{x}=40\), \(s=3.4\):
\[
t=\frac{40-41}{3.4/\sqrt{68}}\approx -2.43.
\]

The raw platform dump showed `-2.43` marked incorrect while also showing a P-value range of \(0.005\le P\text{-value}<0.0075\). That range is inconsistent with the visible screenshot values, which give a left-tail P-value around \(0.009\). Keep this flagged for review if the platform still rejects the statistic.
