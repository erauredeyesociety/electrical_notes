# Question 12

It is claimed that automobiles are driven on average more than 18,000 kilometers per year. To test this claim, 110 randomly selected automobile owners are asked to keep a record of the kilometers they travel. Would you agree with this claim if the random sample showed an average of 18,330 kilometers and a standard deviation of 3700 kilometers? Use a P-value in your conclusion.

Identify the null and alternative hypotheses.

Choices:

- A. \(H_0:\mu \ne 18000,\ H_a:\mu=18000\)
- B. \(H_0:\mu=18000,\ H_a:\mu<18000\)
- C. \(H_0:\mu<18000,\ H_a:\mu=18000\)
- D. \(H_0:\mu=18000,\ H_a:\mu>18000\)
- E. \(H_0:\mu>18000,\ H_a:\mu=18000\)
- F. \(H_0:\mu>18000,\ H_a:\mu=18000\)

## Cleaned / corrected parts

The screenshot reads **18,330** kilometers, not 18,230.

- Correct hypothesis choice: **D**
  - \(H_0:\mu=18000\)
  - \(H_a:\mu>18000\)
- This is a one-sample **t** test because the population standard deviation is not given.

The test statistic is
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
  =\frac{18330-18000}{3700/\sqrt{110}}
  \approx 0.93542.
\]

Rounded to two decimals, enter **0.94**.

With \(df=109\), the right-tail P-value is about \(0.176\). Since this is not small, do not reject \(H_0\). There is not enough evidence to agree with the claim that automobiles are driven more than 18,000 km per year on average.
