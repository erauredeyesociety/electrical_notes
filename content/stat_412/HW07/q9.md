# Question 9

A marketing expert for a pasta-making company believes that 40% of pasta lovers prefer lasagna. If 12 out of 20 pasta lovers choose lasagna over other pastas, what can be concluded about the expert's claim? Use a 0.05 level of significance.

The binomial probability sums table for \(n=19\) and \(n=20\) is available in `9.md`.

Let a success be a pasta lover who chooses lasagna. Identify the null and alternative hypotheses.

Choices:

- A. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p<0.4\)
- B. \(H_0:p\ne0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)
- C. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p\ne0.4\)
- D. \(H_0:p<0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)
- E. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p>0.4\)
- F. \(H_0:p>0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)

What is the appropriate conclusion for this test?

- A. Reject \(H_0\) and conclude that there is sufficient evidence that the percentage of pasta lovers that prefer lasagna is not 40%.
- B. Do not reject \(H_0\) and conclude that there is not sufficient evidence that the percentage of pasta lovers that prefer lasagna is not 40%.
- C. Do not reject \(H_0\) and conclude that there is sufficient evidence that the percentage of pasta lovers that prefer lasagna is not 40%.
- D. Reject \(H_0\) and conclude that there is not sufficient evidence that the percentage of pasta lovers that prefer lasagna is not 40%.

## Answer

- Hypotheses: choice **C**, \(H_0:p=0.4\), \(H_a:p\ne0.4\).
- Under \(H_0\), \(X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n=20,p=0.4)\).
- Since \(x=12\) is greater than \(np_0=8\), MyStatLab's two-tailed exact-binomial rule uses
  \[
  P\text{-value}=2P(X\ge12)=2(0.056526367)\approx0.1131.
  \]
- Since \(0.1131>0.05\), do not reject \(H_0\).
- Conclusion choice: **B**.
