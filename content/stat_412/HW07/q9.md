# Question 9

A marketing expert for a pasta-making company believes that 40% of pasta lovers prefer lasagna. If 12 out of 20 pasta lovers choose lasagna over other pastas, what can be concluded about the expert's claim? Use a 0.08 level of significance.

Let a success be a pasta lover that chooses lasagna over other pastas. Identify the null and alternative hypotheses.

Choices:

- A. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p<0.4\)
- B. \(H_0:p\ne0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)
- C. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p\ne0.4\)
- D. \(H_0:p<0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)
- E. \(H_0:p=0.4,\ H_a:p>0.4\)
- F. \(H_0:p>0.4,\ H_a:p=0.4\)

## Cleaned / confirmed parts

- Correct hypothesis choice: **C**
  - \(H_0:p=0.4\)
  - \(H_a:p\ne0.4\)
- The test statistic is a binomial random variable \(X\), where \(X\) counts the number of pasta lovers who choose lasagna.
- Under \(H_0\), \(X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n=20,p=0.4)\).
  - Enter \(p=0.4\)
  - Enter \(n=20\)

For \(x=12\), the two-sided exact binomial P-value is
\[
P(X\ge 12)+P(X\le 4)\approx 0.1075.
\]

At \(\alpha=0.08\), \(0.1075>0.08\), so do not reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence that the true lasagna preference proportion differs from 0.40.
