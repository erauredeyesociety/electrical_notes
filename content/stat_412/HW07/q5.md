# Question 5

An electrical firm manufactures light bulbs that have a lifetime that is approximately normally distributed with a mean of 800 hours and a standard deviation of 37 hours. Test the hypothesis that \(\mu=800\) hours against the alternative \(\mu\ne800\) hours if a random sample of 33 bulbs has an average life of 781 hours. Use a P-value in your answer.

Find the test statistic. Round to two decimal places.

Later visible parts ask for the P-value and conclusion.

## Cleaned / confirmed parts

The population standard deviation is known, so use a \(z\) test:
\[
z=\frac{781-800}{37/\sqrt{33}}\approx -2.95.
\]

Platform-confirmed:

- Test statistic: **-2.95**
- P-value: **0.003**

Because the P-value is small:

- **Reject** \(H_0\).
- There **is** sufficient evidence to conclude that the mean lifetime of the lightbulbs is not equal to 800 hours.
- In general, \(H_0\) would be rejected for significance levels **greater** than the P-value.
