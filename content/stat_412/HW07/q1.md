# Question 1

Suppose that an allergist wishes to test the hypothesis that at least 31% of the public is allergic to some cheese products. Explain how the allergist could commit the following types of errors.

(a) A type I error.

(b) A type II error.

## Cleaned / confirmed parts

Use the claim as the null hypothesis:

- \(H_0:\ p\ge 0.31\), meaning 31% or more are allergic.
- \(H_a:\ p<0.31\), meaning less than 31% are allergic.

### Part (a): Type I error

A type I error means rejecting a true null hypothesis.

Platform-confirmed fill-ins:

The allergist could conclude that **less than 31%** of the public are allergic to some cheese products when, in fact, **31% or more** are allergic.

### Part (b): Type II error

A type II error means failing to reject a false null hypothesis. In the platform's wording, this is stated as concluding the null-side statement when the alternative is actually true.

Correct fill-ins:

The allergist could conclude that **31% or more** of the public are allergic to some cheese products when, in fact, **less than 31%** are allergic.
