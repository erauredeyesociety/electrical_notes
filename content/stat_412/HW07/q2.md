# Question 2

The proportion of adults living in a small town who are college graduates is estimated to be (p=0.4). To test this hypothesis, a random sample of 15 adults is selected. If the number of college graduates in the sample is anywhere from 3 to 9, we shall not reject the null hypothesis that (p=0.4); otherwise, we shall conclude that (p\ne0.4). Complete parts (a) through (c).

## Answers

Let (X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(15,p)). The non-rejection region is (3\le X\le9).

(a) When (p=0.4),

\[
\alpha=P(X\le2)+P(X\ge10)=0.0609.
\]

(b) The Type II error probabilities are:

- At (p=0.3): (\beta=P(3\le X\le9)=0.8695).
- At (p=0.5): (\beta=P(3\le X\le9)=0.8454).

(c) This **is not** a good test procedure because, although (\alpha) is relatively small, at least one (\beta) is relatively large. In fact, both (\beta) values are large.
