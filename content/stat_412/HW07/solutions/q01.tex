\section*{STAT 412 HW07 --- Question 1}

\subsection*{Problem}

\begin{quote}
Suppose that an allergist wishes to test the hypothesis that at least 31\% of the public is allergic to some cheese products. Explain how the allergist could commit a type I error and a type II error.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Solution}

The platform wording treats the tested claim as ``31\% or more.'' A type I error means concluding the claim is true when it is actually false:

\[
\text{conclude }p\ge0.31\quad\text{when actually }p<0.31.
\]

A type II error means missing the claim when it is actually true:
\[
\text{conclude }p<0.31\quad\text{when actually }p\ge0.31.
\]

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
Type I: conclude 31\% or more are allergic when actually less than 31\% are allergic. Type II: conclude less than 31\% are allergic when actually 31\% or more are allergic.
\end{minipage}}
