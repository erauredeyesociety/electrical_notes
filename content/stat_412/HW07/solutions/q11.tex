\section*{STAT 412 HW07 --- Question 11}

\subsection*{Problem}

\begin{quote}
In the American Heart Association journal \textit{Hypertension}, researchers report that individuals who practice Transcendental Meditation (TM) lower their blood pressure significantly. If a random sample of 240 male TM practitioners meditate for 10.5 hours per week with a standard deviation of 2.22 hours, does that suggest that, on average, men who use TM meditate more than 10 hours per week? Quote a P-value in your conclusion.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Solution}

The phrase ``more than 10 hours'' tells us the alternative is right-tailed:
\[
H_0:\mu=10,\qquad H_1:\mu>10.
\]
So the $H_0$ dropdown is ``equals,'' and the $H_1$ dropdown is ``greater than.'' This is not a ``not equal'' test.

Because the sample standard deviation is used, use a $t$ statistic:
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
  =\frac{10.5-10}{2.22/\sqrt{240}}
  \approx 3.4892.
\]

With $df=240-1=239$, the right-tail P-value is
\[
P(T_{239}>3.4892)\approx 0.0003.
\]
If the platform asks for three decimals, enter $0.000$.

In general, reject $H_0$ for significance levels greater than the P-value.

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
$H_0:\mu=10$; $H_1:\mu>10$. Test statistic $t=3.49$. P-value $\approx0.0003$; reject $H_0$. There is sufficient evidence that male TM practitioners meditate more than 10 hours per week on average. Reject for significance levels greater than the P-value.
\end{minipage}}
