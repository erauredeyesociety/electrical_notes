\section*{STAT 412 HW07 --- Question 5}

\subsection*{Problem}

\begin{quote}
An electrical firm manufactures light bulbs that have a lifetime that is approximately normally distributed with a mean of 800 hours and a standard deviation of 37 hours. Test the hypothesis that $\mu=800$ hours against the alternative $\mu\ne800$ hours if a random sample of 33 bulbs has an average life of 781 hours. Use a P-value in your answer.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Solution}

The population standard deviation is known, so use a $z$ test:
\[
z=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}
  =\frac{781-800}{37/\sqrt{33}}
  \approx -2.9499.
\]
Rounded to two decimals:
\[
z=-2.95.
\]

The alternative is two-sided, so
\[
P\text{-value}=2P(Z<-2.9499)\approx 0.003.
\]

Since the P-value is small, reject $H_0$.

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
$z=-2.95$; P-value $=0.003$. Reject $H_0$; there is sufficient evidence that the mean lifetime is not equal to 800 hours. Reject $H_0$ for significance levels greater than the P-value.
\end{minipage}}
