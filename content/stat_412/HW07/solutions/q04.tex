\section*{STAT 412 HW07 --- Question 4}

\subsection*{Problem}

\begin{quote}
A research report claims that mice with an average life span of 32 months will live to be about 41 months old when 40\% of the calories in their diet are replaced by vitamins and protein. Is there any reason to believe that $\mu<41$ if 68 mice that are placed on this diet have an average life of 40 months with a standard deviation of 3.4 months? Use a P-value in your conclusion.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Solution}

The question asks whether there is reason to believe $\mu<41$, so the alternative is left-tailed:
\[
H_0:\mu=41,\qquad H_a:\mu<41.
\]
This is choice E.

Using the visible screenshot values,
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
  =\frac{40-41}{3.4/\sqrt{68}}
  \approx -2.4254.
\]
Rounded to two decimals:
\[
t=-2.43.
\]

With $df=67$, the exact left-tail P-value is about $0.009$. From the t table, this falls in the range
\[
0.0075<P\text{-value}<0.01.
\]

Because the P-value is less than $0.05$, reject $H_0$.

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
Choice E: $H_0:\mu=41$; $H_a:\mu<41$. $t=-2.43$; $0.0075<P\text{-value}<0.01$ (exact about 0.009). Reject $H_0$; there is sufficient evidence that the mice will live to be less than 41 months old.
\end{minipage}}
