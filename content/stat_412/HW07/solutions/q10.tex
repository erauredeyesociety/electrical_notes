\section*{STAT 412 HW07 --- Question 10}

\subsection*{Problem}

\begin{quote}
Aflatoxins produced by mold on peanut crops must be monitored. A sample of 49 batches of peanuts reveals levels of 24.36 ppm, on average, with a variance of 4.35 ppm. Test the hypothesis that $\sigma^2=4.1$ ppm against the alternative that $\sigma^2\ne4.1$ ppm. Use a P-value in your conclusion.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Solution}

Use a chi-square test for the variance:
\[
H_0:\sigma^2=4.1,\qquad H_a:\sigma^2\ne4.1.
\]

The test statistic is
\[
\chi^2=\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2}
      =\frac{48(4.35)}{4.1}
      \approx 50.9268.
\]
Rounded to two decimals:
\[
\chi^2=50.93.
\]

The degrees of freedom are
\[
df=n-1=48.
\]

Because the alternative is two-sided, the P-value is twice the smaller chi-square tail area. The platform-confirmed rounded value is
\[
P\text{-value}=0.718.
\]

Since $0.718>0.05$, the result is not significant.

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
$\chi^2=50.93$; P-value $=0.718$. The test statistic is not significant at the 0.05 level. Fail to reject $H_0$; there is not sufficient evidence that $\sigma^2\ne4.1$.
\end{minipage}}
