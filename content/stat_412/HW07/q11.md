# Question 11

In the American Heart Association journal *Hypertension*, researchers report that individuals who practice Transcendental Meditation (TM) lower their blood pressure significantly. If a random sample of 240 male TM practitioners meditate for 10.5 hours per week with a standard deviation of 2.22 hours, does that suggest that, on average, men who use TM meditate more than 10 hours per week? Quote a P-value in your conclusion.

State the null and alternative hypotheses.

## Cleaned / clarified parts

The phrase **more than 10 hours per week** means the alternative is right-tailed.

- \(H_0: \mu = 10\)
  - dropdown relation: **equals**
- \(H_1: \mu > 10\)
  - dropdown relation: **greater than**

This is **not** a two-tailed “not equal” test.

Using
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
  =\frac{10.5-10}{2.22/\sqrt{240}}
  \approx 3.49,
\]
with \(df=239\), the right-tail P-value is about \(0.0003\), which rounds to \(0.000\) if the platform asks for three decimals.

Conclusion: reject \(H_0\). The data support that male TM practitioners meditate more than 10 hours per week on average.
