# Question 6

A simple random sample of front-seat occupants involved in car crashes is obtained. Among 2876 occupants not wearing seat belts, 31 were killed. Among 7647 occupants wearing seat belts, 17 were killed. Use a 0.01 significance level to test the claim that seat belts are effective in reducing fatalities.

Let occupants not wearing seat belts be population 1 and occupants wearing seat belts be population 2.

## Hypotheses

- \(H_0:p_1=p_2\)
- \(H_a:p_1>p_2\)

## Solution

\[
\hat p_1=\frac{31}{2876}=0.01078,\qquad
\hat p_2=\frac{17}{7647}=0.00222.
\]

\[
z=5.80,\qquad p\approx0.0000.
\]

Reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that the fatality rate is lower for those wearing seat belts, so the data support the claim that seat belts reduce fatalities.

## Part (b): appropriate confidence interval

For the right-tailed test at \(\alpha=0.01\), use an appropriate 98% confidence interval:

\[
0.0039 < p_1-p_2 < 0.0132.
\]

Because the confidence interval is entirely above 0, there appears to be a difference between the fatality proportions. There is sufficient evidence to support the claim that the fatality rate is higher for those not wearing seat belts.
