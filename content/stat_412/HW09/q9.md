# Question 9

Test the given claim. Identify the null hypothesis, alternative hypothesis, test statistic, P-value, and then state the conclusion about the null hypothesis as well as the final conclusion that addresses the original claim.

Among 2120 passenger cars in a particular region, 245 had only rear license plates. Among 375 commercial trucks, 56 had only rear license plates. A reasonable hypothesis is that commercial truck owners violate laws requiring front license plates at a higher rate than owners of passenger cars. Use a 0.01 significance level to test that hypothesis.

Let passenger cars be population 1 and commercial trucks be population 2.

## Hypotheses

- \(H_0:p_1=p_2\)
- \(H_a:p_1<p_2\)

## Solution

\[
\hat p_1=\frac{245}{2120}=0.1156,\qquad
\hat p_2=\frac{56}{375}=0.1493.
\]

\[
z=-1.85,\qquad p=0.0321.
\]

Fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence to support the claim that commercial truck owners violate the front-plate law at a higher rate.

An appropriate 98% confidence interval for \(p_1-p_2\) is

\[
-0.0795 < p_1-p_2 < 0.0120.
\]

Because the interval contains 0, it agrees with the hypothesis-test conclusion.

## Platform confidence-interval conclusion

- Dropdown options: **is entirely above** / **is entirely below** / **contains**. Select **contains**.
- Dropdown options: **is** / **is not**. Select **is not**.
- Dropdown options: **is** / **is not**. Select **is not**.
- Completed sentence: Because the confidence interval **contains** 0, there **is not** a significant difference between the two proportions. There **is not** sufficient evidence to support the claim that commercial truck owners violate laws requiring front license plates at a higher rate than owners of passenger cars.

Detailed reason: A confidence interval containing 0 means plausible values for \(p_1-p_2\) include no difference at all. Since the claim requires commercial trucks to have the higher violation rate, the interval does not give enough support for that one-sided claim.
