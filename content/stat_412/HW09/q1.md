# Question 1

In a study to estimate the proportion of residents in a certain city and its suburbs who favor the construction of a nuclear power plant, it is found that 53 of 80 urban residents favor the construction while only 59 of 100 suburban residents are in favor. Is there a significant difference between the proportions of urban and suburban residents who favor construction of the nuclear plant? Make use of a P-value.

Let urban residents be population 1 and suburban residents be population 2.

## Hypotheses

- \(H_0:p_1=p_2\)
- \(H_a:p_1\ne p_2\)

## Solution

\[
\hat p_1=\frac{53}{80}=0.6625,\qquad
\hat p_2=\frac{59}{100}=0.5900.
\]

\[
\hat p=\frac{53+59}{80+100}=0.6222.
\]

\[
z=\frac{0.6625-0.5900}{\sqrt{0.6222(1-0.6222)(1/80+1/100)}}=1.00.
\]

The two-tailed P-value is \(p=0.3188\). Since the P-value is large, fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence of a difference between the two proportions.

## Platform conclusion

- Dropdown options: **is not** / **is**. Select **is not**.
- Dropdown options: **greater** / **less**. Select **greater**.
- Completed sentence: Do not reject \(H_0\) and conclude that there **is not** a significant difference between the proportions of urban and suburban residents who favor construction of the nuclear plant. In general, \(H_0\) would be rejected for significance levels **greater** than the P-value.
