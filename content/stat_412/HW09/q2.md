# Question 2

In a study on the fertility of married women, two groups of childless wives aged 25 to 29 were selected at random, and each was asked if she eventually planned to have a child. One group was selected from among wives married less than two years and the other from among wives married five years or longer. Suppose that 251 of the 300 wives married less than two years planned to have children some day compared to 308 of the 400 wives married five years or longer. Can we conclude that the proportion of wives married less than two years who planned to have children is significantly higher than the proportion of wives married five years? Make use of a P-value.

Let wives married less than two years be population 1 and wives married five years or longer be population 2.

## Hypotheses

- \(H_0:p_1=p_2\)
- \(H_a:p_1>p_2\)

## Solution

\[
\hat p_1=\frac{251}{300}=0.8367,\qquad
\hat p_2=\frac{308}{400}=0.7700.
\]

\[
\hat p=\frac{251+308}{300+400}=0.7986.
\]

\[
z=\frac{0.8367-0.7700}{\sqrt{0.7986(1-0.7986)(1/300+1/400)}}=2.18.
\]

The right-tailed P-value is \(0.0148\). Since \(0.0148<0.05\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that the proportion is higher for wives married less than two years.

## Platform conclusion

- Dropdown options: **Reject** / **Do not reject**. Select **Reject** \(H_0\).
- Dropdown options: **is** / **is not**. Select **is**.
- Dropdown options: **can** / **cannot**. Select **can**.
- Completed sentence: Reject \(H_0\). There **is** sufficient evidence to reject the hypothesis that the two proportions are equal. Therefore, it **can** be concluded that the proportion of wives married less than two years who planned to have children is significantly higher than the proportion of wives married five years.
