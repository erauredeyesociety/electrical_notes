\section*{STAT 412 QZ07 --- Question 6}

\subsection*{Problem}

A research report claims that mice with an average life span of 32 months will live to be about 41 months old when 40\% of the calories in their diet are replaced by vitamins and protein. Is there any reason to believe that the true mean $\mu<41$ if 70 mice on this diet have an average life of 40 months with a standard deviation of 3.4 months? Use a P-value in the conclusion.

\subsection*{Solution}

The question asks whether the mean is less than 41:
\[
H_0:\mu=41,\qquad H_1:\mu<41.
\]
This is choice F.

Use a one-sample $t$ test:
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}
=\frac{40-41}{3.4/\sqrt{70}}
=-2.46.
\]

The degrees of freedom are $df=70-1=69$. For a left-tailed test,
\[
P=P(T_{69}\le -2.46)\approx 0.0082.
\]
From the $t$ table, this falls in the range
\[
0.0075<P\text{-value}<0.01.
\]

Since the P-value is small, reject $H_0$.

\subsection*{Final Answer}

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
Choice F. $t=-2.46$; $0.0075<P\text{-value}<0.01$. Reject $H_0$; there is sufficient evidence that the mice will live to be less than 41 months old.
\end{minipage}}
