# Question 6

A research report claims that mice with an average life span of 32 months will live to be about 41 months old when 40% of the calories in their diet are replaced by vitamins and protein. Is there any reason to believe that the true mean \(\mu<41\) if 70 mice that are placed on this diet have an average life of 40 months with a standard deviation of 3.4 months? Use a P-value in your conclusion.

The table of critical values of the t-distribution is available in `6.md`.

Identify the null and alternative hypotheses.

A. \(H_0:\mu=41\), \(H_1:\mu>41\)

B. \(H_0:\mu\ne41\), \(H_1:\mu=41\)

C. \(H_0:\mu>41\), \(H_1:\mu=41\)

D. \(H_0:\mu=41\), \(H_1:\mu\ne41\)

E. \(H_0:\mu<41\), \(H_1:\mu=41\)

F. \(H_0:\mu=41\), \(H_1:\mu<41\)

Find the test statistic.

Round to two decimal places as needed.

Determine the range into which the P-value falls.

What is the appropriate conclusion for this test?

\[
\_\ H_0
\]

and conclude that there \(\_\) sufficient evidence that mice with an average life span of 32 months will live to be \(\_\) than 41 months old when 40% of the calories in their diet are replaced by vitamins and protein.

## Answer

Hypotheses: choice F.

Test statistic: \(t=-2.46\).

P-value range: \(0.0075 < P\text{-value} < 0.01\) (exact \(P\approx0.0082\)).

Reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that the mice will live to be less than 41 months old.
