# Question 19

The number of hours of reserve capacity of 10 randomly selected automotive batteries is shown.

Data:

`1.75, 1.84, 1.58, 1.65, 1.73, 1.95, 1.36, 1.56, 1.46, 2.05`

Assume the sample is taken from a normally distributed population. Construct 99% confidence intervals for (a) the population variance \(\sigma^2\) and (b) the population standard deviation \(\sigma\).

## Cleaned / confirmed parts

The sample variance is approximately
\[
s^2\approx0.04636,
\]
with
\[
df=n-1=9.
\]

Using the chi-square confidence interval formula,
\[
\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_R}<\sigma^2<\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_L}.
\]

Platform-confirmed variance interval:

- \(\sigma^2\) CI: **\((0.018,0.240)\)**

Platform-confirmed variance interpretation:

- Choice **B**
- With **99%** confidence, it can be said that the population variance is between **0.018** and **0.240**.

Important entry note: the upper variance endpoint is **0.240**, not `24`. The newest raw HTML confirms the platform accepted `0.24`; using `0.240` is the clean three-decimal version.

Part (b) asks for the confidence interval for the population standard deviation. Take square roots of the variance interval endpoints, preferably from the unrounded variance limits:

\[
\sqrt{0.017686}\approx0.133,\qquad \sqrt{0.240476}\approx0.490.
\]

Thus:

- \(\sigma\) CI: **\((0.133,0.490)\)**

If asked for the standard-deviation interpretation, it should be the 99% confidence statement that the population standard deviation is between **0.133** and **0.490** hours.
