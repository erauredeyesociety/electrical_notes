# Question 2

Refer to the data in the accompanying table for the heights of females. Complete parts (a) through (d). The height classes are:

| Height class (cm) |
|---|
| Less than 156.05 |
| 156.05-162.15 |
| 162.15-168.25 |
| Greater than 168.25 |

Use a 0.10 significance level to test the claim that the heights were randomly selected from a normally distributed population. For the normal model in parts (b) and (c), use the sample mean and sample standard deviation.

## Answer

The sample has

\[
n=147,\qquad \bar{x}=161.6875,\qquad s=7.4838.
\]

Observed frequencies:

| Height class (cm) | Observed frequency |
|---|---:|
| Less than 156.05 | 32 |
| 156.05-162.15 | 40 |
| 162.15-168.25 | 47 |
| Greater than 168.25 | 28 |

Normal probabilities using \(\bar{x}=161.6875\) and \(s=7.4838\):

| Height class (cm) | Probability | Expected frequency |
|---|---:|---:|
| Less than 156.05 | 0.2256 | 33.17 |
| 156.05-162.15 | 0.2990 | 43.95 |
| 162.15-168.25 | 0.2851 | 41.91 |
| Greater than 168.25 | 0.1903 | 27.97 |

Platform expected-frequency table entries, left to right:

\[
33.17,\quad 43.95,\quad 41.91,\quad 27.97.
\]

\[
H_0:\text{for each height class, the observed frequency equals the corresponding expected frequency.}
\]

\[
H_1:\text{for at least one height class, the observed frequency does not equal the corresponding expected frequency.}
\]

Because the mean and standard deviation are estimated from the sample, use

\[
df=4-1-2=1.
\]

\[
\chi^2=1.02,\qquad p=0.314.
\]

Since \(p>0.10\), fail to reject \(H_0\). There is insufficient evidence at the 0.10 level to warrant rejection of the claim that the female heights were randomly selected from a normally distributed population.
