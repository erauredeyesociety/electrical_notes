# Question 5

An experiment was conducted to determine whether giving candy to dining parties resulted in greater tips. The mean tip percentages and standard deviations are given with the sample sizes. Assume independent simple random samples from normally distributed populations, and do not assume equal population standard deviations.

| Group | Parameter | \(n\) | \(\bar{x}\) | \(s\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No candy | \(\mu_1\) | 25 | 18.14 | 1.46 |
| Two candies | \(\mu_2\) | 25 | 20.58 | 2.34 |

Use a 0.01 significance level to test the claim that giving candy results in greater tips. Then construct a confidence interval suitable for testing the claim.

## Answer

Because the claim is that two candies produce greater tips, compare \(\mu_1-\mu_2\):

\[
H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2,\qquad H_1:\mu_1<\mu_2.
\]

Using Welch's two-sample \(t\) test,

\[
t=-4.42,\qquad df\approx40.23,\qquad p=0.000.
\]

Since \(p<0.01\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence to support the claim that giving candy results in greater tips.

A two-sided confidence interval suitable for the one-tailed 0.01 test is the 98% confidence interval:

\[
-3.78<\mu_1-\mu_2<-1.10.
\]

Platform confidence-interval blanks:

- Lower blank: \(-3.78\)
- Upper blank: \(-1.10\)

This supports the hypothesis-test conclusion because the entire interval is below 0.
