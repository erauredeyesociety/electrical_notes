# Question 8

Engineers at a large automobile manufacturing company are trying to decide whether to purchase brand A or brand B tires. An experiment is conducted using 12 tires of each brand. The tires are run until they wear out. Test the hypothesis that there is no difference in the average wear of the two brands of tires. Assume the populations are approximately normally distributed with equal variances. Use a P-value.

Let Brand A be population 1 and Brand B be population 2.

| Brand | \(n\) | \(\bar{x}\) kilometers | \(s\) kilometers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 12 | 38,100 | 5,000 |
| B | 12 | 40,100 | 5,900 |

The second image for this question is `8-2.png`, which contains the experiment-results popup.

## Answer

\[
H_0:\mu_1-\mu_2=0,\qquad H_1:\mu_1-\mu_2\ne0.
\]

Using the pooled two-sample \(t\) test,

\[
s_p=5468.55,\qquad t=-0.90,\qquad df=22.
\]

The exact P-value is approximately \(0.380\), so the table range is

\[
0.3<P\text{-value}<0.4.
\]

Do not reject \(H_0\). There is insufficient evidence to conclude that there is a difference in the average wear of the two brands of tires.
