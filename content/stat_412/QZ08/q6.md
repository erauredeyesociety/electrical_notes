# Question 6

The table includes results from polygraph experiments. In each case, it was known whether the subject lied or did not lie. Use a 0.05 significance level to test the claim that whether a subject lies is independent of the polygraph test indication. Do the results suggest that polygraphs are effective in distinguishing between truth and lies?

|  | Did not lie | Lied |
|---|---:|---:|
| Polygraph indicated lie | 15 | 49 |
| Polygraph indicated no lie | 7 | 9 |

## Answer

\[
H_0:\text{whether a subject lies is independent of the polygraph test indication.}
\]

\[
H_1:\text{whether a subject lies is not independent of the polygraph test indication.}
\]

Expected counts:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{Did not lie} & \text{Lied}\\ \hline
\text{Indicated lie} & 17.6 & 46.4\\
\text{Indicated no lie} & 4.4 & 11.6
\end{array}
\]

Platform expected-count table, if requested:

|  | Did not lie | Lied |
|---|---:|---:|
| Polygraph indicated lie | 17.6 | 46.4 |
| Polygraph indicated no lie | 4.4 | 11.6 |

\[
\chi^2=2.649,\qquad df=1,\qquad p=0.1036.
\]

Since \(p>0.05\), fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence to warrant rejection of the claim that whether a subject lies is independent of the polygraph test indication. These results do not suggest that polygraphs are effective in distinguishing between truth and lies.
