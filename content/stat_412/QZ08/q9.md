# Question 9

A study was done using a treatment group and a placebo group. Assume independent simple random samples from normally distributed populations, and do not assume equal population standard deviations. Use a 0.01 significance level for both parts.

| Group | Parameter | \(n\) | \(\bar{x}\) | \(s\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Treatment | \(\mu_1\) | 35 | 2.32 | 0.91 |
| Placebo | \(\mu_2\) | 39 | 2.62 | 0.56 |

## Part (a)

Test the claim that the two samples are from populations with the same mean.

## Part (b)

Construct a confidence interval suitable for testing the claim that the two samples are from populations with the same mean.

## Answer

\[
H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2,\qquad H_1:\mu_1\ne\mu_2.
\]

Using Welch's two-sample \(t\) test,

\[
t=-1.68,\qquad df\approx55.32,\qquad p=0.098.
\]

Since \(p>0.01\), fail to reject \(H_0\). There is not sufficient evidence to warrant rejection of the claim that the two samples are from populations with the same mean.

The matching two-sided confidence interval is the 99% confidence interval:

\[
-0.775<\mu_1-\mu_2<0.175.
\]

Platform confidence-interval blanks:

- Lower blank: \(-0.775\)
- Upper blank: \(0.175\)

The interval contains 0, so it supports the hypothesis-test conclusion.
