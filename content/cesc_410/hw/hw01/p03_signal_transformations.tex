\problemheaderlo{3}{LO02}{20 pts, 5 pts each}{Plotting DT signal transformations}

\subsection*{Problem statement}

Consider the following DT signal
\begin{equation}
  x[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    \cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}n\right), & 0 \le n \le 3 \\[8pt]
    0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:xn}
\end{equation}
Plot the signal and its variations using the plotting format shown in the class
notes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $x[n]$
  \item $x[n-2]$
  \item $x[-n]$ \quad {\small(flip concept, LO 5)}
  \item $x[2-n]$
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Approach}

Every one of these is the same signal with its \emph{index} rewritten, so the
safest method is to tabulate the four nonzero samples once and then ask, for
each transformation, \emph{which output index reproduces each original sample}.

From \eqref{eq:xn}, the signal is nonzero only at $n = 0,1,2,3$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
  \toprule
  $n$ & $\cos(\pi n/6)$ & value \\
  \midrule
  $0$ & $\cos 0$        & $1$ \\
  $1$ & $\cos(\pi/6)$   & $\sqrt{3}/2 \approx 0.866$ \\
  $2$ & $\cos(\pi/3)$   & $1/2 = 0.5$ \\
  $3$ & $\cos(\pi/2)$   & $0$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Note $x[3] = 0$ exactly. It is still \emph{in} the support of the signal, but it
plots as a stem of zero height, so the visible nonzero samples are only three.

The two rules needed:
\begin{align}
  x[n - n_0] &: \text{shift RIGHT by } n_0 \text{ (delay)} \label{eq:shift}\\
  x[-n]      &: \text{FLIP about } n = 0 \label{eq:flip}
\end{align}

The reliable way to handle a combined case like $x[2-n]$ is to substitute rather
than reason about ``flip then shift'' verbally, where it is easy to shift the
wrong direction. If $y[n] = x[2-n]$, then $y[n]$ takes the value $x[m]$ where
$m = 2-n$, i.e. $n = 2-m$. So each original sample at index $m$ lands at output
index $2-m$.

\subsection*{Work}

\paragraph{1. $x[n]$.}
The signal as given: samples $1$, $0.866$, $0.5$, $0$ at $n = 0,1,2,3$.

\begin{center}
\stemplot{-4}{6}{(-4,0)(-3,0)(-2,0)(-1,0)(0,1)(1,0.866)(2,0.5)(3,0)(4,0)(5,0)(6,0)}
\end{center}

\paragraph{2. $x[n-2]$.}
By \eqref{eq:shift} this delays the signal by two samples: whatever was at index
$m$ moves to $m+2$. Support moves from $[0,3]$ to $[2,5]$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
  \toprule
  original $m$ & value & lands at $n = m+2$ \\
  \midrule
  $0$ & $1$     & $2$ \\
  $1$ & $0.866$ & $3$ \\
  $2$ & $0.5$   & $4$ \\
  $3$ & $0$     & $5$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\stemplot{-4}{6}{(-4,0)(-3,0)(-2,0)(-1,0)(0,0)(1,0)(2,1)(3,0.866)(4,0.5)(5,0)(6,0)}
\end{center}

\paragraph{3. $x[-n]$.}
By \eqref{eq:flip}, sample at index $m$ lands at $-m$. Support moves from
$[0,3]$ to $[-3,0]$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
  \toprule
  original $m$ & value & lands at $n = -m$ \\
  \midrule
  $0$ & $1$     & $0$  \\
  $1$ & $0.866$ & $-1$ \\
  $2$ & $0.5$   & $-2$ \\
  $3$ & $0$     & $-3$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\stemplot{-6}{4}{(-6,0)(-5,0)(-4,0)(-3,0)(-2,0.5)(-1,0.866)(0,1)(1,0)(2,0)(3,0)(4,0)}
\end{center}

\paragraph{4. $x[2-n]$.}
Using the substitution above, sample at index $m$ lands at $n = 2-m$. Support
moves from $[0,3]$ to $[-1,2]$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
  \toprule
  original $m$ & value & lands at $n = 2-m$ \\
  \midrule
  $0$ & $1$     & $2$  \\
  $1$ & $0.866$ & $1$  \\
  $2$ & $0.5$   & $0$  \\
  $3$ & $0$     & $-1$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\stemplot{-5}{5}{(-5,0)(-4,0)(-3,0)(-2,0)(-1,0)(0,0.5)(1,0.866)(2,1)(3,0)(4,0)(5,0)}
\end{center}

Equivalently, $x[2-n] = x[-(n-2)]$: flip first (part 3), then delay the
\emph{flipped} signal by 2. Checking against part 3, whose support was
$[-3,0]$: delaying by 2 gives $[-1,2]$, which matches.

\subsection*{Check}

Verify one sample of each transform directly from the definition, rather than
trusting the tables:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
  \toprule
  Transform & Direct evaluation & Plot \\
  \midrule
  $x[n-2]$ at $n=2$  & $x[0] = 1$        & $1$ \checkmark \\
  $x[-n]$  at $n=-1$ & $x[1] = 0.866$    & $0.866$ \checkmark \\
  $x[2-n]$ at $n=0$  & $x[2] = 0.5$      & $0.5$ \checkmark \\
  $x[2-n]$ at $n=2$  & $x[0] = 1$        & $1$ \checkmark \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A further structural check: a flip preserves the number of samples and reverses
their order, while a shift preserves order. Parts 3 and 4 both read
$0,\,0.5,\,0.866,\,1$ left to right — reversed relative to parts 1 and 2, as a
flip requires. Parts 1 and 2 both read $1,\,0.866,\,0.5,\,0$.

\subsection*{Answer}

\begin{answerbox}
All four are the same four samples $\{1,\ 0.866,\ 0.5,\ 0\}$ relocated:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
  \toprule
  Signal & Support & Samples (left to right) \\
  \midrule
  $x[n]$    & $0 \le n \le 3$   & $1,\ 0.866,\ 0.5,\ 0$ \\
  $x[n-2]$  & $2 \le n \le 5$   & $1,\ 0.866,\ 0.5,\ 0$ \\
  $x[-n]$   & $-3 \le n \le 0$  & $0,\ 0.5,\ 0.866,\ 1$ \\
  $x[2-n]$  & $-1 \le n \le 2$  & $0,\ 0.5,\ 0.866,\ 1$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{answerbox}
