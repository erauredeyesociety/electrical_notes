\problemheaderlo{2}{LO01}{10 pts, 5 pts each}{Phasor arithmetic}

\subsection*{Problem statement}

Consider the signals of the previous problem. Find the following using the
phasor technique:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $x_a(t) + x_b(t)$
  \item $x_b(t) - x_c(t)$
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Approach}

This is the reason phasors exist. Adding two sinusoids of the same frequency in
the time domain requires trigonometric identities; adding their phasors is
ordinary complex arithmetic. The procedure is always the same three steps:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Convert each phasor from polar to rectangular form,
    $\phasor{A}{\phi} \to A\cos\phi + jA\sin\phi$.
  \item Add or subtract the rectangular components.
  \item Convert back to polar, $\phasor{\sqrt{\Real^2+\Imag^2}}{\operatorname{atan2}(\Imag,\Real)}$.
\end{enumerate}

The phasors come straight from Problem 1:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_a = \phasor{10}{-75^\circ}, \qquad
  \mathbf{X}_b = \phasor{15}{15^\circ},  \qquad
  \mathbf{X}_c = \phasor{12}{75^\circ}.
\end{equation}

This works \emph{only} because all three share the same $\omega$. Sinusoids at
different frequencies cannot be combined this way.

\subsection*{Work}

\paragraph{1. $x_a(t) + x_b(t)$.}
To rectangular form:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{X}_a &= 10\cos(-75^\circ) + j\,10\sin(-75^\circ)
                = 2.5882 - j\,9.6593, \\
  \mathbf{X}_b &= 15\cos(15^\circ)  + j\,15\sin(15^\circ)
                = 14.4889 + j\,3.8823.
\end{align}
Add componentwise:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_a + \mathbf{X}_b = (2.5882 + 14.4889) + j(-9.6593 + 3.8823)
                              = 17.0771 - j\,5.7770.
\end{equation}
Back to polar:
\begin{align}
  |\mathbf{X}| &= \sqrt{17.0771^2 + 5.7770^2} = \sqrt{325} = 18.0278, \\
  \angle\mathbf{X} &= \arctan\!\left(\frac{-5.7770}{17.0771}\right) = -18.69^\circ .
\end{align}

There is a shortcut worth noticing. The two phases differ by exactly
$15^\circ - (-75^\circ) = 90^\circ$, so the phasors are \emph{orthogonal} and the
magnitude follows from Pythagoras without any component arithmetic:
\begin{equation}
  |\mathbf{X}_a + \mathbf{X}_b| = \sqrt{10^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{325} = 5\sqrt{13},
\end{equation}
with the angle measured from $\mathbf{X}_a$:
$-75^\circ + \arctan(15/10) = -75^\circ + 56.31^\circ = -18.69^\circ$. Same answer,
and it shows the result is exact rather than a rounded decimal.

\paragraph{2. $x_b(t) - x_c(t)$.}
To rectangular form:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{X}_b &= 14.4889 + j\,3.8823, \\
  \mathbf{X}_c &= 12\cos(75^\circ) + j\,12\sin(75^\circ) = 3.1058 + j\,11.5911.
\end{align}
Subtract componentwise:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_b - \mathbf{X}_c = (14.4889 - 3.1058) + j(3.8823 - 11.5911)
                              = 11.3831 - j\,7.7088.
\end{equation}
Back to polar:
\begin{align}
  |\mathbf{X}| &= \sqrt{11.3831^2 + 7.7088^2} = \sqrt{189} = 13.7477, \\
  \angle\mathbf{X} &= \arctan\!\left(\frac{-7.7088}{11.3831}\right) = -34.11^\circ .
\end{align}

Here the phases differ by $60^\circ$, so the law of cosines gives the exact
magnitude:
\begin{equation}
  |\mathbf{X}_b - \mathbf{X}_c|^2 = 15^2 + 12^2 - 2(15)(12)\cos 60^\circ
    = 369 - 180 = 189,
\end{equation}
so $|\mathbf{X}| = \sqrt{189} = 3\sqrt{21}$.

\subsection*{Check}

Converting back to the time domain and evaluating at $\omega t = 0$, where
$x(0) = A\cos\phi$, must reproduce the sum of the originals at $t = 0$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
  \toprule
  & From the originals & From the phasor \\
  \midrule
  $x_a(0) + x_b(0)$ & $2.5882 + 14.4889 = 17.0771$ & $18.0278\cos(-18.69^\circ) = 17.0771$ \\
  $x_b(0) - x_c(0)$ & $14.4889 - 3.1058 = 11.3831$ & $13.7477\cos(-34.11^\circ) = 11.3831$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both agree. Note that this check verifies the \emph{real part} only; the exact
magnitudes $\sqrt{325}$ and $\sqrt{189}$ derived geometrically above confirm the
rest independently.

\subsection*{Answer}

\begin{answerbox}
\begin{align*}
  x_a(t) + x_b(t) &\;\longleftrightarrow\; \phasor{5\sqrt{13}}{-18.69^\circ}
    = \phasor{18.03}{-18.69^\circ} \\[2pt]
  &\quad\Rightarrow\; 18.03\cos(\omega t - 18.69^\circ) \\[6pt]
  x_b(t) - x_c(t) &\;\longleftrightarrow\; \phasor{3\sqrt{21}}{-34.11^\circ}
    = \phasor{13.75}{-34.11^\circ} \\[2pt]
  &\quad\Rightarrow\; 13.75\cos(\omega t - 34.11^\circ)
\end{align*}
\end{answerbox}
